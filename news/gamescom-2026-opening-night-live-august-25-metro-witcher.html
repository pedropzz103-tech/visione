Gamescom 2026 Opening Night Live starts August 25 with Metro 2039, Witcher updates and more

The world’s largest gaming event is days away, and organizers are already promising world premieres plus updates on Metro 2039 and The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt — Songs of the Past.

By VISIONE Wire · August 19, 2026 · 19:42 CEST

Gamescom Opening Night Live returns on August 25, 2026, one day before the main Cologne show opens, with organizers promising new announcements, world premieres and updates from across the games industry.

The show will again be hosted by Geoff Keighley, with Eefje “sjokz” Depoortere as co-host. Gamescom’s organizers have already confirmed that the presentation will include news on Metro 2039 and The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt — Songs of the Past, both currently expected in 2027.

A bigger-than-ever gamescom

Organizers say all available exhibition space is booked for 2026, a first for the event. Confirmed companies span Nintendo, Xbox, Ubisoft, Electronic Arts, Capcom, CD Projekt Red, Bandai Namco, Sega, HoYoverse, Krafton, Tencent Games, NetEase and many more.

Gamescom runs from August 26 through August 30 in Cologne, with August 26 serving as the trade visitor and media day. Gamescom dev begins August 24.

Opening Night Live is the traffic engine

For people watching from home, Opening Night Live is the key moment. The event is designed around reveals and globally streamed announcements, which means every new trailer or surprise can instantly create search demand for release dates, platforms, gameplay details and pre-orders.

Gamescom is also expanding its digital and community programming this year. Games Done Quick is making its European gamescom debut, while the event is increasing its indie-show presence and creator programming.

What to watch for

The confirmed Metro and Witcher updates are only the starting point. With so many major publishers on the exhibitor list, the event is positioned to generate multiple days of follow-up stories rather than a single night of news.

VISIONE Wire will treat gamescom as a live news cycle, with individual stories for major reveals rather than one giant roundup that buries the interesting parts.
