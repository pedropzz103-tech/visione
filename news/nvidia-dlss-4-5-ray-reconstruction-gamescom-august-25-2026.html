Nvidia releases DLSS 4.5 Ray Reconstruction with Gamescom RTX push

A second-generation transformer model for ray-traced image reconstruction is available now through the Nvidia app, while Gamescom brings new RTX commitments for 007 First Light, Gears of War: E-Day, Control Resonant and more.

By VISIONE Wire · August 25, 2026 · 20:00 CEST

Nvidia has released DLSS 4.5 Ray Reconstruction, extending its latest neural-rendering stack with a second-generation transformer model designed to improve image quality in ray-traced and path-traced games. The update arrived on August 25 alongside a broad Gamescom 2026 push covering new RTX integrations, game bundles, cloud-gaming features and developer technology.

The release matters because Ray Reconstruction is not another frame-generation setting. It replaces conventional hand-tuned denoisers used to clean up incomplete ray-traced samples with an AI model trained to reconstruct a more stable high-resolution image. Nvidia says the new version brings more compute, a larger model and better use of temporal and spatial engine data while maintaining performance close to the previous generation.

As with any vendor performance or quality claim, the improvements described by Nvidia are company-reported. The practical result will vary by game, scene, resolution, hardware and implementation, and independent comparisons will determine how large the upgrade feels in real play.

What changed in DLSS 4.5 Ray Reconstruction

Nvidia says the second-generation Ray Reconstruction transformer has 35% more compute capability and processes 20% more parameters than the prior model. The company also says it was trained on a larger dataset and has deeper spatial awareness, with the goal of reducing familiar ray-tracing artifacts such as unstable reflections, flickering lighting and ghosting in motion.

The system continues to combine denoising and reconstruction rather than treating them as separate stages. In simple terms, a ray-traced game cannot afford to calculate every possible light ray for every pixel. It renders a sparse sample and then reconstructs the missing information. Nvidia is betting that a transformer trained on large amounts of rendered data can make better decisions about that missing information than traditional rules-based filters.

Nvidia also says developers now get finer control over temporal accumulation, which governs how much information from previous frames is carried into the current image. That is a technical detail with visible consequences: too much historical information can create trails or ghosting, while too little can make lighting noisy or unstable.

Available now, but initially through Early Access

The wording around availability deserves precision. Nvidia says the new Ray Reconstruction model is available now through a new Nvidia app update, but users need to opt into Early Access releases under Settings and About. A broader official release is planned for September. GeForce Game Ready Driver 580.88 WHQL or newer is also required.

Once the update is installed, users can open the Graphics section in the Nvidia app, select a supported game or global settings, and choose the recommended Ray Reconstruction preset. Nvidia lists 30 games that can use the override at launch, including Cyberpunk 2077, Alan Wake 2, Indiana Jones and the Great Circle, Doom: The Dark Ages, Marvel's Spider-Man 2, Resident Evil Requiem and Portal with RTX.

That means the technology is arriving with a meaningful installed base rather than waiting for future games alone. Developers can also add native support through game updates, which should allow title-specific tuning rather than relying solely on the app-level override.

Gamescom turns the release into a broader RTX showcase

Nvidia paired the software release with 11 Gamescom announcements. The company says 007 First Light will receive path tracing and DLSS 4.5 Ray Reconstruction on September 15. Control Resonant, due September 24, is being shown with path tracing and DLSS 4.5 and is now part of a bundle with selected GeForce RTX graphics cards, laptops and PCs.

Gears of War: E-Day is also getting RTX Mega Geometry alongside DLSS 4.5. Nvidia says the technology is intended to improve ray tracing of dense geometry, a particularly relevant problem in modern Unreal Engine scenes. The game will also support Nvidia Reflex and the wider DLSS suite on GeForce RTX systems.

Other Gamescom titles highlighted by Nvidia include AION 2, ANANTA, Aniimo, Beautiful Light, Black State, CINDER CITY, Fragmentary Order and Silver Palace. The specific feature mix differs by title, so the announcement should not be read as every game supporting every RTX technology.

Why Ray Reconstruction is strategically important

DLSS has evolved from a resolution-upscaling tool into a collection of neural-rendering systems that increasingly influence the entire rendered frame. Super Resolution reconstructs a higher-resolution output from a lower-resolution input. Frame Generation inserts additional frames. Dynamic Multi Frame Generation can vary how many generated frames are used. Ray Reconstruction replaces conventional denoising in ray-traced workloads.

For Nvidia, that software layer is a competitive moat around GeForce hardware. Raw shader and ray-tracing performance still matter, but the company increasingly sells the experience as a combination of hardware, drivers, trained models and developer integrations. The more games that are designed around that stack, the harder it becomes to compare GPUs using only traditional rasterization metrics.

The approach also shifts some graphics progress from fixed-function silicon to models that can be updated after a GPU has shipped. Owners of existing RTX hardware can receive a new reconstruction model without replacing the card, although the performance ceiling remains determined by the underlying GPU.

What is confirmed, and what still needs testing

Confirmed by Nvidia: the second-generation DLSS 4.5 Ray Reconstruction model is available through the Nvidia app Early Access channel; it requires driver 580.88 WHQL or newer; Nvidia lists 30 games that can use the override; and a wider official release is planned for September.

Company claims: the new model has 35% more compute capability, 20% more parameters and improved temporal stability and lighting accuracy. Those statements come from Nvidia's own technical material and demonstrations.

Still to be established independently: the magnitude of image-quality improvement across different games, the performance cost in difficult scenes, and whether the newer model consistently reduces artifacts without introducing new ones. Those questions are best answered by frame-by-frame third-party testing.

What comes next

The immediate next step is broader testing as players install the Early Access update and compare it across the 30 supported titles. September brings both the wider Nvidia app release and the promised 007 First Light path-tracing update. Control Resonant follows on September 24, while AION 2's western launch is scheduled for September 30.

Gamescom will add more technical detail too. Nvidia says an Xbox Gamescom broadcast on August 26 will discuss Gears of War: E-Day's use of ray tracing on full-detail Nanite objects with developers from The Coalition.

Bottom line

DLSS 4.5 Ray Reconstruction is a meaningful step in Nvidia's strategy to make neural rendering a standard part of PC graphics rather than an optional upscaler. The new model is available now to Early Access users, reaches a substantial list of existing games and arrives with a fresh wave of RTX commitments around Gamescom.

The headline numbers are promising, but image reconstruction is judged on details that marketing slides cannot settle: motion, fine geometry, reflections, particle effects and stability from one frame to the next. The next few weeks of independent testing will show whether the second-generation transformer delivers the visible leap Nvidia is promising.
