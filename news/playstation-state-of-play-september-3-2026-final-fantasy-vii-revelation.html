PlayStation State of Play September 3, 2026: start time, State of Play Japan and Final Fantasy VII Revelation

Sony has scheduled two PlayStation broadcasts back-to-back on Thursday, September 3. The first will mix PlayStation Studios and third-party updates before ending with an extended look at Final Fantasy VII Revelation; a Japan-focused show follows immediately.

By VISIONE Wire · August 31, 2026 · 18:11 CEST

Sony has confirmed a new State of Play for September 3, 2026, and this time the event is effectively a double feature. PlayStation says the main State of Play will begin at 6:00 a.m. Pacific / 9:00 a.m. Eastern / 3:00 p.m. Central European Summer Time / 10:00 p.m. Japan Standard Time, followed immediately by a separate State of Play Japan focused on creators in Japan and across Asia.

The announcement gives players a concrete schedule but deliberately leaves much of the lineup unspoiled. Sony says the first broadcast will include updates and announcements from PlayStation Studios and third-party partners, then conclude with an extended look at Square Enix's Final Fantasy VII Revelation. State of Play Japan will then shift the emphasis toward games from Japanese and Asian studios, with gameplay, announcements and deeper dives.

What is confirmed: both broadcasts take place Thursday, September 3; the first begins at 6:00 a.m. PT / 9:00 a.m. ET / 3:00 p.m. CEST / 10:00 p.m. JST; State of Play Japan follows immediately; Final Fantasy VII Revelation closes the first show with an extended segment. Sony has not published a full game list, total runtime or a complete rundown of announcements.

State of Play September 3 start time

For viewers in North America, the show starts unusually early on the West Coast. The official start times are 6:00 a.m. PT and 9:00 a.m. ET. In Central Europe, including Spain, France, Germany and Italy, the broadcast begins at 3:00 p.m. CEST. In Japan, the start time is 10:00 p.m. JST.

Sony says both shows will air live on its official YouTube and Twitch channels. The main State of Play will be presented in English with Japanese subtitles, while State of Play Japan will be presented in Japanese with English subtitles. That language setup makes the second program unusually accessible outside Japan compared with many region-specific showcases.

What Sony has actually promised

The wording of the announcement is broad but useful. Sony specifically names both PlayStation Studios and third-party partners for the first show, which means the program is not framed as a single-publisher event or a showcase built around one franchise. The safest expectation is a mixed slate of first-party and external games, but anything beyond that remains speculation until Sony or the publishers involved confirm it.

The clearest anchor is Final Fantasy VII Revelation. Sony says the first broadcast will conclude with an extended look at the game's “vast world,” making Square Enix's title the only named game in the announcement. Revelation was highlighted again during gamescom in late August, where PlayStation described it as targeting Spring 2027 on PS5 and showed Cloud and his allies travelling aboard the Highwind while the Weapons emerged as a major threat.

That recent trailer gives the September 3 segment a useful context. Square Enix has already shown that Revelation is moving beyond a simple teaser phase, so an “extended look” could reasonably mean a longer gameplay demonstration or a more detailed systems overview. That is analysis, not a confirmed format: Sony has not said exactly how long the segment will run or whether new release details will be announced.

State of Play Japan returns immediately after

The second broadcast is not merely a translated replay. State of Play Japan is a separate program, again hosted by Japanese voice actor Yuki Kaji, and Sony says it will focus on upcoming games from studios in Japan and Asia. The format promises gameplay, announcements and deeper dives, which gives the second show its own editorial identity rather than making it an appendix to the global State of Play.

This split also lets Sony serve two different audiences in one block. The main show can carry globally marketed PlayStation titles and major partners, while the Japan program can spend more time on games that may have stronger regional roots or need longer demonstrations for an international audience.

Why the timing matters

The event lands just over a week after gamescom Opening Night Live and only days into the September release calendar. That creates a useful window for publishers to clarify release dates, show follow-up gameplay and push preorders or demos without having to compete directly with the Cologne show's first wave of announcements.

For PlayStation specifically, the September 3 showcase also arrives during a busy stretch of already announced software. Marvel's Wolverine is scheduled for September 15, while the platform's autumn calendar includes other major third-party launches and ongoing PS Plus updates. A State of Play in this window can therefore serve both immediate releases and games positioned for 2027.

The important distinction is that Sony has not promised that every major PlayStation title will appear. State of Play events often mix large franchises with smaller projects, regional games, updates and release-date announcements. Treating the absence of a named title in advance as evidence that it will or will not appear would be guesswork.

How to watch

Sony directs viewers to the official PlayStation channels on YouTube and Twitch. At the time of publication, the announcement page confirms those destinations, but a stable event-specific YouTube watch URL was not surfaced in the official materials available to VISIONE Wire. For that reason, this article links to the official PlayStation streams page rather than embedding an unverified or third-party restream.

Watch via PlayStation's official YouTube streams page. Sony also lists Twitch as an official destination in its announcement.

Co-streaming warning from Sony

Sony included a practical warning for creators who plan to co-stream or archive the presentation. The company says licensed music and other copyrighted material may appear in the broadcast and may be outside Sony Interactive Entertainment's control. That can create problems for saved VODs, clips or recap videos even when live co-streaming is otherwise welcomed.

For ordinary viewers this changes nothing, but for channels preserving a replay it matters. Sony specifically advises creators to omit copyrighted music when possible if they intend to save the broadcast as a VOD or reuse clips later.

What to watch for next

The biggest immediate question is the rest of the lineup. Sony has named Final Fantasy VII Revelation but has not identified other first-party or third-party games, which leaves room for substantial announcements without making any particular rumor credible.

The second question is whether the event produces release-date movement. Gamescom established or reinforced several 2026 and 2027 windows across PlayStation's broader ecosystem, and September is a natural point for publishers to convert broad windows into exact dates.

Finally, Revelation itself is worth watching for scale rather than surprise. Square Enix has already established the Highwind, the Weapons and a Spring 2027 target in recent official material. The value of the September 3 segment will be in how much more clearly it explains exploration, combat, world structure and the final chapter's scope.

For now, the schedule is simple: one global State of Play, one Japan-focused companion broadcast, and Final Fantasy VII Revelation as the confirmed closing act of the first show. Everything else belongs in the “wait for the stream” column.
