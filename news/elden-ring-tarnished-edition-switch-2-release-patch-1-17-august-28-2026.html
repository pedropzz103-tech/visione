GAMING / NINTENDO SWITCH 2 / ELDEN RING

Elden Ring Tarnished Edition launches on Switch 2 with patch 1.17 and new classes

FromSoftware's first Nintendo version of Elden Ring arrives August 28 as an all-in package with Shadow of the Erdtree, new starting classes, armor, weapons and Torrent customization, while existing platforms receive Tarnished Pack support through patch 1.17.

By VISIONE Wire · Published August 28, 2026 · 08:00 CEST

Elden Ring Tarnished Edition launches for Nintendo Switch 2 on August 28, bringing FromSoftware's 2022 action RPG and the Shadow of the Erdtree expansion to Nintendo hardware for the first time. The release also lands alongside patch 1.17 on PlayStation, Xbox and PC, which adds support for the separately sold Tarnished Pack and makes broader balance adjustments.

The Switch 2 edition is positioned as the complete package rather than a stripped-down port. Nintendo and Bandai Namco list the base game, Shadow of the Erdtree, new armor, two additional starting classes and three appearance options for Torrent, the player's spectral steed. Nintendo's European listing puts the download size at 41 GB and confirms handheld, tabletop and TV modes, HDR support and online play for up to six players in supported modes.

For players who already own Elden Ring on PlayStation 4, PlayStation 5, Xbox One, Xbox Series X|S or PC, the new content is being sold separately as the Tarnished Pack. Bandai Namco's patch notes for version 1.17 say the update is required to support that DLC when it becomes available on August 28.

What is included in Tarnished Edition

The most important distinction is that Tarnished Edition is not merely Elden Ring running on a new device. It bundles the original game with Shadow of the Erdtree, FromSoftware's major 2024 expansion, and folds the new Tarnished Pack content into the Switch 2 release by default.

Nintendo's official launch-day material says the added content includes four new armor sets, two new starting classes and three cosmetic variants for Torrent. Bandai Namco's patch 1.17 notes identify the new classes as the Idus Knight and Heavy Knight. Their starting equipment is not permanently locked to those classes: the same gear can also be acquired from merchants or found in the Lands Between.

The patch notes also confirm additional weapons tied to the Tarnished Pack. That matters because the pack changes more than character creation. Existing characters can obtain the added equipment without beginning a new save, which makes the DLC relevant to veteran players as well as newcomers arriving through Switch 2.

Patch 1.17 prepares existing platforms for the new DLC

Bandai Namco released patch 1.17 on August 27 for PlayStation 4, PlayStation 5, Xbox One, Xbox Series X|S and Steam. Its primary role is enabling the Tarnished Pack, but the update also includes gameplay balance adjustments.

The company explicitly advises players to install the latest update before playing. Because the Switch 2 version includes the Tarnished Pack content as part of Tarnished Edition, Nintendo owners do not need to buy that add-on separately.

This split release strategy gives FromSoftware a way to introduce Switch 2-specific packaging without isolating the new classes, equipment and Torrent cosmetics from the game's existing audience. In practice, August 28 is both a platform expansion and a content refresh across the broader Elden Ring ecosystem.

The Switch 2 version includes the full Shadow of the Erdtree expansion

Shadow of the Erdtree remains a major part of the value proposition. The expansion sends players to the Land of Shadow and builds a separate late-game progression path around Miquella, new regions, bosses, weapons, spells and dungeons.

Nintendo is presenting Tarnished Edition as an entry point for people who may have missed the game during its original PlayStation, Xbox and PC run. Its Japanese launch article spends substantial space explaining the Cave of Knowledge tutorial, mounted travel with Torrent, spirit summons, weapon skills and cooperative multiplayer, signaling that the company expects the Switch 2 release to attract first-time players rather than only existing fans buying another copy.

The platform's portable mode is the obvious differentiator. Elden Ring has previously been playable away from a television through devices such as PC handhelds and remote-play setups, but Tarnished Edition is the first official Nintendo version built around a hybrid console.

What is confirmed about performance

Nintendo and Bandai Namco have confirmed the feature set and release date, but they have not published a detailed technical specification sheet covering every performance target in each Switch 2 mode. The official product pages emphasize the complete content package, HDR compatibility and hybrid play rather than promising a specific frame rate or resolution.

That distinction is important because early hands-on impressions from Gamescom have raised questions about performance in the open world, particularly when compared with more powerful consoles and PC hardware. Those impressions are useful context, but they are not a substitute for final retail-version testing across docked and handheld modes.

For that reason, VISIONE Wire is treating frame-rate quality as an open question until final-version measurements are available. The confirmed fact today is that the game is shipping on Switch 2, not that it matches the performance profile of the PS5, Xbox Series X or a high-end PC.

Why the launch matters for Nintendo Switch 2

Elden Ring is one of the defining games of the current console generation. Nintendo's European store notes more than 30 million copies sold worldwide, while the game has accumulated major Game of the Year awards and a large long-term player base. Bringing it to Switch 2 gives Nintendo another high-profile third-party release that was absent from the original Switch.

It also tests a central promise of the newer hardware: that publishers can bring more demanding modern games to Nintendo's hybrid ecosystem without relying on cloud streaming. Tarnished Edition is therefore relevant beyond FromSoftware's audience. Its technical reception will be watched as another signal of how effectively Switch 2 can handle large open-world games designed around stronger baseline hardware.

What players get on August 28

Confirmed: Elden Ring Tarnished Edition releases for Nintendo Switch 2 on August 28, 2026; it includes the base game, Shadow of the Erdtree and Tarnished Pack content; the Tarnished Pack adds Idus Knight and Heavy Knight starting classes, new weapons, armor and three Torrent appearance options; patch 1.17 adds DLC support and balance changes on PlayStation, Xbox and PC.

Also confirmed: the Switch 2 version supports portable, tabletop and TV play, HDR on compatible displays and online features that require Nintendo Switch Online where applicable. Nintendo lists the game at 41 GB in Europe.

Still uncertain: the exact final performance profile across all Switch 2 scenarios. Players interested primarily in technical parity should wait for retail-version testing rather than extrapolate from prerelease demonstrations.

Bottom line

Elden Ring's August 28 Switch 2 release is more than a late port. Tarnished Edition packages the full game and its largest expansion with a new slice of content that is simultaneously being made available to the existing player base.

For newcomers, it is now the most self-contained version on Nintendo hardware. For existing players, patch 1.17 and the Tarnished Pack provide a smaller reason to return. The bigger question is technical: whether FromSoftware can translate the scale and atmosphere of the Lands Between cleanly enough to make portable Elden Ring one of Switch 2's most convincing third-party showcases.
