Salesforce and Anthropic launch Claudeforce with 37 AI sales skills

The expanded partnership brings live Salesforce data, workflows and governed business actions directly into Claude, while Salesforce makes Anthropic its preferred AI assistant across much of its own workforce.

By VISIONE Wire · Published August 27, 2026 · 18:00 CEST

Salesforce and Anthropic have expanded their partnership with a new initiative called Claudeforce, starting with a Salesforce-in-Claude plugin that gives sellers 37 prebuilt skills for working with live CRM data and taking governed actions from inside Claude.

The announcement, made alongside Salesforce's fiscal second-quarter results, is one of the clearest examples yet of a major enterprise software company preparing for a world in which users may spend less time clicking through conventional application interfaces and more time asking an AI agent to retrieve information, reason across systems and execute work on their behalf.

Salesforce says the first product, Salesforce in Claude, is already available to select pilot customers and is expected to enter open beta in September 2026. Additional prebuilt skills are planned for later in the year.

What Claudeforce actually does

Claudeforce is not a single new model. It is the name Salesforce and Anthropic are using for a deeper integration between Claude and Salesforce's data, workflows, business logic, actions and governance layer.

The first implementation is a plugin with 37 sales-focused skills. Salesforce says those skills include meeting preparation, deal-health review and pipeline review. The system can reason over revenue context and then perform actions such as updating a pipeline, but those actions are routed through Salesforce so the company's existing permissions and business rules remain in force.

That distinction is important. Enterprise adoption of agentic AI is not only a model-quality problem. Companies also need to know which data an agent can read, which actions it can take, how permissions are inherited and whether existing compliance controls continue to apply. Salesforce is pitching its own infrastructure as the deterministic layer around Claude's probabilistic reasoning.

Salesforce says the connection is enabled by AIforce, a framework that exposes enterprise data and workflows to agents through MCP servers, APIs and command-line tools. In practical terms, the company wants Claude to become another interface to the same underlying business system rather than a separate chatbot that requires a parallel integration project.

Salesforce is testing a different future for software interfaces

The strategic idea behind the deal is larger than sales automation. For decades, enterprise software has been organized around fixed screens, menus, forms and dashboards. An employee who wants an answer may have to open several records, compare history and manually decide what to do next.

With Claudeforce, Salesforce and Anthropic are betting that an agent can absorb more of that navigation layer. A seller could ask Claude to assess a deal, pull relevant CRM records and Slack context, generate a live view of the account, then take a permitted action without manually visiting every underlying screen.

Salesforce describes this shift aggressively: the company says software can become a system that powers many interfaces rather than a single fixed interface. The claim still has to survive real deployment, especially around accuracy, latency, user trust and governance, but the architecture points toward a significant change in how SaaS products may be used.

There is also an incentive for Salesforce to embrace this transition rather than resist it. Investors have spent much of 2026 debating whether frontier AI agents could weaken traditional software companies by replacing portions of their user interfaces or automating workflows that previously required paid seats. Claudeforce is Salesforce's counterargument in product form: even if the interface becomes an AI agent, the underlying enterprise data model, permissions, workflows and transaction systems can remain valuable.

Claude is becoming deeply embedded inside Salesforce

The partnership runs both ways. Salesforce says Claude is now the default model for Slack AI, Slackbot, Salesforce in Claude, Headless 360 and Agentforce Coworker, while Claude Code is being used across Salesforce's engineering organization.

Salesforce also plans to make Claude Code and Claude Enterprise available to all of its developers and knowledge workers as the company's preferred AI assistant and productivity tools. It says Anthropic is the first large-language-model provider fully integrated within the Salesforce Trust Boundary.

Those moves give Anthropic a strategically important enterprise distribution channel. Rather than competing only as a standalone assistant, Claude is being embedded into Salesforce products and into the daily work of Salesforce's own employees.

For Salesforce, the arrangement broadens its model strategy while keeping the workflow and governance layer under its own control. That matters because enterprise customers increasingly want access to more than one frontier model, but they may not want to rebuild security, identity and data connections separately for each provider.

The announcement landed with a strong earnings report

Claudeforce arrived as Salesforce reported revenue of $11.3 billion for its fiscal second quarter ended July 31, up 11% year over year. Subscription and support revenue reached $10.8 billion, while current remaining performance obligations rose 14% year over year to $33.5 billion.

The company also raised its fiscal 2027 revenue outlook to a range of $46.1 billion to $46.4 billion. Operating cash flow rose 71% to $1.3 billion and free cash flow increased 81% to $1.1 billion.

Reuters reported that Salesforce shares jumped 14% in extended trading after the company raised its annual forecasts and announced the expanded Anthropic partnership. The market reaction cannot be attributed to Claudeforce alone: the quarter also included stronger earnings, guidance and capital-return factors. Still, the timing tied the product announcement directly to the broader question of whether AI is becoming an accelerator or a threat for established software companies.

What is confirmed, and what is still unproven

Confirmed: Salesforce in Claude has 37 prebuilt sales skills; pilot access is live for selected customers; open beta is expected in September 2026; more skills are planned later in 2026; and Salesforce says Claude will be the preferred AI assistant for its developers and knowledge workers.

Also confirmed: actions taken through the integration are intended to respect Salesforce permissions and business rules rather than bypass them, and the companies are planning additional integrations across Claude, Salesforce and Slack.

Not yet established: how well the system performs across large, messy enterprise deployments; the rate at which users will trust agents to change live CRM records; the pricing economics at scale; and whether agentic interfaces materially reduce demand for traditional software seats or instead increase usage of the underlying platform.

Salesforce's marketing language is unsurprisingly bullish, and claims about a new era of enterprise software should be treated as strategy rather than settled fact. The more concrete signal is architectural: one of the world's largest SaaS vendors is making its core platform available through an external AI interface and designing governance around that interaction from the start.

Why this partnership matters beyond Salesforce

Claudeforce is part of a wider contest over the control point in enterprise AI. Model companies want to become the place where users start work. Software vendors want to preserve the value of their data, permissions and domain-specific workflows. Cloud providers want the infrastructure layer. And businesses want flexibility without creating a new security stack for every assistant.

The Salesforce-Anthropic arrangement tries to split those roles cleanly. Claude handles reasoning and conversational interaction. Salesforce supplies trusted business context, action execution and governance. If that model works, it could become a template for other enterprise software vendors that would rather expose their systems to AI agents than force customers to choose between old interfaces and new assistants.

It could also accelerate adoption of the Model Context Protocol and similar standards. Salesforce specifically cites MCP servers as part of AIforce, giving agents a structured way to discover and use tools. The more large platforms support those interfaces, the easier it becomes for agents to work across multiple enterprise systems without bespoke integration code for every pairing.

What happens next

The next milestone is the planned September open beta for Salesforce in Claude. That will expand the test beyond selected pilot customers and provide a better view of how the 37 skills behave in real sales organizations.

Salesforce and Anthropic also plan to add more prebuilt skills later in 2026 and broaden integrations across Slack and other Salesforce surfaces. Pricing and packaging may change, and availability can vary by region and customer agreement.

The bigger question will take longer to answer: whether enterprise software becomes less visible as AI interfaces become more prominent, or whether the software platforms underneath those agents become even more important because they own the data, rules and actions the agents need.

Bottom line

Claudeforce is a notable enterprise-AI move because it treats Claude not as an add-on chatbot but as a new front end to governed Salesforce workflows. The first release is narrow enough to evaluate, with 37 sales skills and a September beta, but the direction is broad: Salesforce is preparing for a software market in which the interface may increasingly be generated by an AI agent while the underlying CRM remains the system of record.
