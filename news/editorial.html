EDITORIAL STANDARDS

Original value before publishing volume.

VISIONE Wire exists to explain consequential developments in AI, software, computing infrastructure, cybersecurity, digital business and technology policy. Speed matters when a story is moving, but volume is not a target. If a candidate story cannot clear the reporting and original-value gates below, the correct editorial decision is to skip it or wait for better evidence.

The original-value gate

A new article must add something meaningful beyond summarizing or rephrasing source material. That may be a chronology assembled from several sources, a comparison built from source data, a transparent calculation, analysis of a filing or technical specification, a benchmark against earlier events, or a clearly labeled VISIONE Wire interpretation connecting multiple verified facts.

Word count is not a quality metric. A short article with strong evidence is preferable to a padded article written to reach an arbitrary length. A longer analysis is justified only when the reporting supports it.

Sources and verification

For normal news reporting, we prefer a primary source plus at least one credible independent source. Primary sources can include court documents, regulatory filings, technical documentation, company newsrooms, research papers, public datasets and official statements. Independent reporting is used to confirm events, add context and test claims made by interested parties.

Articles distinguish confirmed facts from source claims, allegations, estimates and analysis. Dates, numerical claims, legal conclusions and technical specifications are checked before publication. Substantive reports include a visible Sources section so readers can inspect the evidence.

Updates instead of duplicate pages

When a story develops materially, VISIONE Wire normally updates the existing URL rather than creating another page that repeats the same background. Significant updates should display a modified time or an update note. A new URL should represent a genuinely different development or piece of analysis.

AI assistance

AI tools may assist with research organization, translation, transcription, drafting and editing. They are never treated as a source. AI-generated text does not become publishable merely because it sounds plausible; factual claims still require external evidence and editorial review.

AI assistance must not invent names, quotations, credentials, dates, prices, specifications, sources, legal conclusions or image rights. When a fact cannot be verified, the article should say it is uncertain or leave it out.

Images and video

Relevant official press material, public-domain media, compatible Creative Commons material, government libraries and VISIONE-owned assets are preferred. Credits and licensing context are included where required or useful. We avoid random hotlinks, decorative screenshots, unlicensed fan art and misleading imagery.

Official video may be embedded or linked when it materially improves the report. Third-party video is not downloaded and re-uploaded as if it were VISIONE Wire material.

Headlines

Headlines should be factual, specific and proportional to the evidence. Search visibility matters, but search-friendly wording does not justify deceptive certainty, inflated claims or rumor presented as fact.

Corrections

Material factual errors are corrected promptly. When a correction changes the meaning of a report, the article should disclose that correction. Minor spelling, formatting or broken-link fixes may be made without a formal note.

Commercial separation

Advertising, sponsorships or affiliate relationships do not buy editorial conclusions, favorable headlines, source interpretation or the right to approve independent coverage. Paid material must be clearly labeled. See Advertising & Sponsorship Standards.
