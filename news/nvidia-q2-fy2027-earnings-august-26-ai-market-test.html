Nvidia earnings on August 26 will test the AI rally: what to watch

Nvidia enters its fiscal second-quarter report with a $91 billion revenue guide, record data-center growth and market expectations so large that the result could influence the broader technology trade.

By VISIONE Wire · August 22, 2026 · 12:00 CEST

Nvidia will report results for the second quarter of fiscal 2027 on Wednesday, August 26, in one of the most closely watched corporate events of the summer. The company has already told investors to expect roughly $91 billion in quarterly revenue, plus or minus 2%, after posting record first-quarter sales of $81.6 billion.

The release matters well beyond one company. Nvidia sits at the center of the global artificial-intelligence infrastructure buildout, supplying the accelerators, networking systems and software stack used by cloud companies, model developers, governments and enterprises. Its numbers have therefore become a proxy for a much larger question: is demand for AI compute still accelerating fast enough to justify the extraordinary amount of capital now flowing into data centers?

According to Nvidia's investor-relations announcement, results are expected at about 1:20 p.m. Pacific time on August 26, followed by a conference call at 2 p.m. Pacific, or 5 p.m. Eastern. The quarter ended July 26.

The baseline is already enormous

Nvidia's most recent quarter set an unusually high bar. Fiscal first-quarter revenue reached $81.6 billion, up 20% from the previous quarter and 85% from a year earlier. Data Center revenue was $75.2 billion, up 92% year over year. The company reported GAAP gross margin of 74.9% and non-GAAP gross margin of 75.0%.

For fiscal Q2, management guided to $91.0 billion in revenue, plus or minus 2%, while again assuming no Data Center compute revenue from China in the outlook. The midpoint implies another sizable sequential increase from an already record base.

That is the first number investors will compare with the actual report. A simple earnings beat may not be enough to satisfy the market if the forward outlook suggests slower expansion, weaker margins or delayed infrastructure deployment.

What investors will watch first

The most important issue is Data Center growth. Nvidia's transformation from a graphics-chip company into the core supplier of AI infrastructure is now overwhelmingly reflected in that segment. With Data Center representing the large majority of quarterly sales, even modest changes in deployment schedules at hyperscale customers can have an outsized impact on reported growth.

The second issue is product transition. Nvidia has been moving customers through the Blackwell generation while preparing the Vera Rubin platform and expanding rack-scale systems, networking and related infrastructure. Investors will look for evidence that production, packaging and customer deployments are keeping pace with demand rather than simply shifting revenue between quarters.

The third issue is gross margin. Nvidia's profitability has remained exceptionally high despite rapid product transitions and the complexity of delivering large-scale systems. A meaningful margin change could signal a different mix of products, supply-chain costs, pricing pressure or faster expansion into lower-margin infrastructure components.

China remains a major uncertainty

Nvidia explicitly excluded Data Center compute revenue from China when it issued its Q2 outlook. That makes China policy and sales restrictions one of the clearest areas where confirmed financial results can diverge from broader investor expectations.

The company has repeatedly had to adapt products and revenue assumptions to changing export controls. The immediate question for August 26 is not only whether any China-related revenue appears, but how management describes the size of the accessible market and the financial consequences of restrictions.

Any change in assumptions could affect both near-term sales and the longer-term competitive landscape, particularly as Chinese customers and suppliers pursue domestic alternatives.

The AI financing boom raises the stakes

The earnings report arrives as the financing model behind AI infrastructure is becoming more aggressive. Technology companies, private-credit firms, banks and chip suppliers are increasingly using debt, project finance and long-term guarantees to support enormous data-center projects.

Reuters reported on August 21 that Nvidia is one of the central pillars of an AI-driven stock-market rally now being tested by higher bond yields and tighter financial conditions. The same report noted that semiconductor stocks had come under pressure during the week as investors weighed rising borrowing costs against the scale of future AI spending.

That connection is important. If Nvidia signals that customer demand remains extremely strong, investors may view the recent pressure as a financing problem rather than a demand problem. If growth expectations soften, however, markets could begin questioning whether the capital committed to AI infrastructure is running ahead of near-term economic returns.

Why August 26 could move more than Nvidia

Nvidia is deeply embedded in the spending plans of major cloud and technology companies. Its results can therefore offer clues about capital expenditure at customers building large GPU clusters, inference systems and AI services.

A strong quarter accompanied by a strong outlook would support the argument that AI infrastructure remains one of the fastest-growing categories of global capital spending. It could also strengthen demand expectations for networking suppliers, memory manufacturers, power-equipment companies, data-center operators and chip-design partners.

A weaker outlook would not automatically mean that the AI boom is ending. Quarter-to-quarter results can be affected by product transitions, shipment timing and capacity constraints. But because expectations are so elevated, any slowdown would likely be examined for signs that customers are becoming more selective about new projects.

The market backdrop is unusually sensitive

This earnings cycle also lands beside the Federal Reserve's Jackson Hole gathering, where investors are looking for guidance on the path of interest rates. Higher long-term yields make expensive growth stocks less attractive in valuation models and can increase the cost of financing the data centers required for AI expansion.

Reuters reported that Nvidia earnings and Jackson Hole are now two of the principal tests facing the current stock-market rally. That framing captures why the week is potentially volatile: one event speaks to the strength of corporate AI demand, while the other speaks to the price of capital supporting that demand.

The two forces are increasingly connected. AI infrastructure can remain strategically important while still becoming more expensive to fund.

What is confirmed and what is not

Confirmed: Nvidia will report fiscal Q2 2027 results on August 26. The company guided to $91 billion in revenue, plus or minus 2%. Its first-quarter revenue was $81.6 billion, with $75.2 billion from Data Center. Nvidia's official call is scheduled for 2 p.m. Pacific.

Not yet known: actual Q2 revenue, Data Center growth, gross margin, future-quarter guidance and any updated assumptions around China. Those figures will only become factual once Nvidia releases them.

Market expectations published by analysts and financial media can help explain price moves, but they are not company guidance and should not be treated as confirmed operating results.

What happens next

Immediately after the release, the most useful comparison will be between actual revenue and Nvidia's $91 billion midpoint, followed by the outlook for fiscal Q3. Investors will then focus on Data Center growth, margins, product supply, China and management commentary on customer capital spending.

The broader interpretation may take longer. A single quarter cannot settle the debate over AI economics, but Nvidia's scale means it can reveal whether infrastructure demand is still compounding at a pace consistent with the enormous commitments being made across the industry.

That is why August 26 is more than another earnings date. It is a concentrated test of the assumptions now supporting AI stocks, data-center investment and one of the largest technology capital cycles in history.
