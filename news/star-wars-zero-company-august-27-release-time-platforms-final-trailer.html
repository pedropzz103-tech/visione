Star Wars Zero Company launches August 27: release time, platforms and final trailer

Bit Reactor's single-player tactics game reaches PC, PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S this week, bringing an original squad into the shadows of the Clone Wars.

By VISIONE Wire · August 24, 2026 · 12:04 CEST

Star Wars Zero Company launches on Thursday, August 27, 2026, putting one of the year's most closely watched strategy releases into the middle of gamescom week. Electronic Arts lists the game for PC, PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, with the EA store showing availability from 15:00 UTC on launch day.

The date and platforms are confirmed directly by Electronic Arts and Lucasfilm. The game is developed by Bit Reactor in collaboration with EA and Lucasfilm Games, and it is built as a single-player, turn-based tactics title rather than an action game in the style of the recent Star Wars Jedi series.

That distinction is central to why Zero Company is interesting. It takes the Star Wars license into squad management, tactical positioning, character relationships and consequential battlefield decisions, while setting its original story during the twilight of the Clone Wars.

When does Star Wars Zero Company release?

EA's official store currently lists August 27, 2026 at 15:00 UTC as the availability time. That corresponds to 17:00 CEST in Spain and much of central Europe, 11:00 ET in the eastern United States and 08:00 PT on the US West Coast.

Store timing can sometimes vary by platform or region, so players should treat the platform storefront in their own territory as the final authority if a local countdown differs. The confirmed global date, however, is August 27.

Zero Company is available for PC through the EA app, Steam and Epic Games Store, and on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. EA has not announced a PlayStation 4 or Xbox One edition, and no Nintendo Switch version is part of the confirmed launch lineup.

What kind of Star Wars game is it?

Zero Company is a single-player turn-based tactics game. Players command an elite squad both in combat and from a base of operations called the Den. EA says missions take place across changing battlefields and that decisions can alter how a playthrough develops.

The player character is Hawks, a former officer of the Galactic Republic. Hawks can be customized in appearance and combat specialization, while the wider squad can include both authored characters and custom operatives.

Confirmed archetypes include clone troopers, scoundrels, astromechs, a Mandalorian and a Jedi. The structure therefore resembles the appeal of squad tactics games such as XCOM, but the developers are using Star Wars factions, locations, abilities and character relationships to give the formula a distinct identity.

EA has also emphasized bonds between squad members. Operatives can improve as they deploy together and unlock combat synergies. That makes the social layer more than decoration: relationships are designed to feed back into tactical performance.

The story takes place in the shadows of the Clone Wars

The campaign is set late in the Clone Wars, a period already heavily explored in animation, novels and games but still rich with space for stories outside the main Jedi and clone armies.

Zero Company is recruited to confront the Infinite Coil, a Separatist-aligned paramilitary cult led by Kundri Fathom. Lucasfilm's August 17 final-trailer announcement says Fathom is attempting to spread a deadly plague across the galaxy, pushing the squad into a conflict that operates beneath the larger war.

The developers are mixing original characters with established Star Wars figures. Lucasfilm confirmed at San Diego Comic-Con that Matt Lanter returns as Anakin Skywalker and Dee Bradley Baker voices clone trooper Trick, while the selectable versions of Hawks are voiced by Jonathan Freeman and Erica Luttrell. Rekha Sharma voices Kundri Fathom.

Those appearances do not mean the game is primarily about legacy characters. Everything shown so far positions the new squad and its internal dynamics at the center of the campaign.

The final trailer is now official

Lucasfilm published the game's final trailer on August 17, ten days before launch. It gives a closer look at Kundri Fathom, the Infinite Coil and the campaign's more cinematic sequences, including Zero Company's ship flying into a volcano.

Because the trailer is hosted through Lucasfilm's official Star Wars site, VISIONE is linking to the original rather than downloading or re-uploading third-party video. Watch the official final trailer at StarWars.com.

EA's official Zero Company page also includes the final trailer and additional official gameplay videos explaining operators, enemies and the game's major systems.

What is confirmed about customization and squad building?

Hawks can be personalized in both appearance and combat specialization. The rest of the team can be assembled from original authored operatives and custom-made characters, with loadouts, appearances and abilities adjusted to fit the player's preferred strategy.

EA says tactical choices extend beyond combat. Players will make decisions at the Den, prepare the squad for operations and adapt to an evolving battlefield. The company has also said outcomes can vary between playthroughs.

The key uncertainty is how far those consequences go. Marketing language confirms changing outcomes and squad development, but it does not by itself prove that the campaign has radically different endings or a fully systemic narrative. That distinction will become clearer when the complete game is available and independently reviewed.

Why Zero Company matters during a crowded launch week

The game arrives during an unusually busy final week of August. Xbox's official release roundup for August 24 to 28 places Zero Company among the week's major launches, while gamescom is simultaneously concentrating industry attention on Cologne.

For EA, the release also tests whether a major Star Wars game can succeed outside the action-adventure format that has dominated the publisher's recent premium releases in the franchise. Turn-based tactics is a smaller genre than blockbuster action, but it has a dedicated PC and console audience and naturally fits squad-based Clone Wars storytelling.

For Lucasfilm Games, it is another attempt to broaden the range of Star Wars interactive experiences after years in which the license was associated heavily with a narrower set of publishers and genres.

What to watch after August 27

The first questions after launch will be straightforward: review scores, PC performance, console technical quality, campaign length and the depth of the tactical systems. Strategy players will also be watching difficulty, enemy variety, replayability and whether squad relationships meaningfully alter combat rather than functioning mainly as narrative flavor.

There is also the question of post-launch support. EA's current material is focused on the base release and preorder cosmetics. Players should not assume expansions, new campaigns or additional platforms until those are formally announced.

What is already confirmed is enough to make August 27 a meaningful date for both Star Wars and strategy fans. Zero Company is not trying to be another lightsaber action game. It is betting that commanding a vulnerable, customizable squad in the less visible corners of the Clone Wars can carry a full premium release.
