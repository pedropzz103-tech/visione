Liquid Network pauses after roughly $320 million in bitcoin is withdrawn

The movement of about 4,000 BTC out of Liquid's federation wallet is not just another crypto exploit. It is a stress test for the security assumptions behind a Bitcoin-linked settlement network used by exchanges, trading desks and asset issuers.

By Pedro · September 7, 2026 · 09:05 CEST

Liquid Network has paused new transaction activity after roughly 4,000 bitcoin, worth about $320 million, was withdrawn from its federation wallet by parties describing themselves as white-hat hackers.

Reuters reported on September 7 that the withdrawal represented almost all of the roughly 4,200 BTC held in the federation wallet before the incident. Liquid said the funds moved through SideSwap's Peg-out Authorization Key mechanism and that the relevant cryptographic key itself was not compromised. Blockstream, Liquid's technology provider, is attempting to contact the parties responsible.

What is confirmed: Liquid says approximately 4,000 BTC left the federation wallet, the network has halted new transaction activity, exchanges were asked to suspend LBTC deposits and withdrawals, and the parties behind the transfer identified themselves on-chain as white hats. The root cause has not been publicly established, and the description “white hat” remains a claim by the actors, not an independently verified status.

Why this incident matters

Liquid is a Bitcoin sidechain designed for faster, confidential settlement and the issuance of digital assets. Bitcoin moved into Liquid is represented as LBTC, which is intended to maintain a one-to-one relationship with BTC held on the Bitcoin network.

That design makes the security of the federation wallet central to the system. Blockstream's own documentation describes Liquid as operated by a distributed federation rather than by proof-of-work miners, with functionaries responsible for signing blocks and securing the bitcoin backing the network.

The September 6 withdrawal therefore cuts directly into the network's core trust model. Even if the funds are ultimately returned, the fact that approximately 95% of the reported federation reserve could leave in one incident raises a more important engineering question than the dollar value alone: which control failed, and why did the system not stop the transfer before the reserve was almost emptied?

The key is said to be intact, which makes the unanswered question more serious

Liquid said the withdrawal occurred through SideSwap's Peg-out Authorization Key, or PAK, but that the key was not compromised. Reuters and The Block both reported that point from the network's statement.

That distinction matters. A stolen private key would provide a relatively familiar explanation. If the key remained secure, investigators need to determine whether a software flaw, authorization-path weakness, implementation bug or another control failure allowed the withdrawal to be treated as valid.

The Block reported that Liquid had not yet publicly explained the root vulnerability. It also said bridge nodes were temporarily disabled, effectively pausing the sidechain while federation members worked on the incident.

Why bridge and federation security deserves more attention

Crypto infrastructure has repeatedly shown that the weakest point is often not the underlying blockchain but the systems connecting one environment to another. Bridges, custodial wrappers and federation mechanisms can concentrate risk because they control large pools of assets while exposing additional software and governance layers.

Liquid is structurally different from many smart-contract bridges, but the lesson is similar. A one-to-one peg is only as resilient as the operational machinery protecting the reserve and enforcing withdrawals. If that control plane fails, the cryptographic strength of Bitcoin itself does not automatically protect the sidechain's backing assets.

For institutions evaluating blockchain settlement systems, this incident is likely to sharpen scrutiny around withdrawal authorization, emergency controls, monitoring, federation governance and recovery procedures. The important question is not simply whether losses are eventually avoided. It is whether the system can explain precisely how the event occurred and demonstrate that the same path cannot be repeated.

What users should understand

The incident does not mean Bitcoin's base layer was compromised. The affected infrastructure is Liquid's federation and peg mechanism, not Bitcoin's proof-of-work consensus.

The Block reported that other assets issued on Liquid, including USDT and real-world assets, were described by the network as unaffected by the security incident itself. However, users can still be affected operationally because the sidechain is paused and exchanges have suspended LBTC flows.

That distinction is important for risk analysis. Asset solvency, network availability and bridge security are separate questions. A system can remain economically recoverable while still suffering a severe failure of availability or control.

The “white-hat” label should not be treated as resolution

An on-chain message associated with the incident said, “we are whitehats. contact us on chain.” That message is relevant evidence about the actors' stated intent, but it does not establish motive or guarantee that the bitcoin will be returned.

Liquid and Blockstream are trying to contact the parties involved, and the funds' eventual disposition will determine whether this becomes a large theft, a negotiated security disclosure, or something in between. Until that is resolved, describing the event simply as a white-hat rescue would go beyond the available evidence.

What to watch next

The most consequential next update will be a technical root-cause explanation. Liquid needs to establish how the peg-out was authorized, which software or operational control failed, whether federation governance behaved as designed and what changes are required before normal activity resumes.

A second issue is fund recovery. If the bitcoin is returned under a responsible-disclosure arrangement, the financial damage may be limited. But the security implications will remain, because the ability to move almost the entire federation reserve is itself the event that matters.

Finally, exchanges and institutional users will want evidence that the restart is based on a verified fix rather than simply on restoration of access. For infrastructure that presents itself as a settlement layer, post-incident transparency is part of the product.

Bottom line

The Liquid incident qualifies as high-value infrastructure news because it exposes a potential weakness in a system designed to extend Bitcoin into faster settlement and digital-asset issuance. Roughly $320 million in bitcoin moved, the sidechain was paused, and the root cause is still unknown.

If the funds return, the headline financial risk may fade quickly. The engineering question will not. The durability of Liquid's security model now depends on a clear explanation of how the withdrawal happened and whether the network can prove that its federation controls are stronger after the incident than they were before it.
