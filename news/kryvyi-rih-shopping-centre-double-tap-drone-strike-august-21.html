Kryvyi Rih shopping-centre strike kills at least 16 as Ukraine says rescuers were targeted in second wave

At least 130 people were injured, including 23 children, after two drone strikes hit a busy shopping centre and the emergency response around it. Rescue work continued into August 22.

By VISIONE Wire · August 22, 2026 · 08:00 CEST

At least 16 people were killed and 130 injured after Russian drones struck a shopping centre in Kryvyi Rih on August 21, according to Ukrainian regional officials and international reporting. Ukrainian authorities say the attack came in two waves, with a second drone arriving roughly 30 minutes after the first strike, when emergency crews were already working at the scene.

The casualty count was still being updated overnight. Oleksandr Hanzha, head of the Dnipropetrovsk regional military administration, said rescuers recovered another body from the rubble shortly after midnight, raising the death toll to 16. Local officials said 23 children were among the injured and 29 people were in serious condition, including five children.

Because rescue and debris-clearing operations were still under way early on August 22, the numbers may change. VISIONE Wire is treating the latest official Ukrainian figures as provisional rather than final.

What is confirmed

Ukrainian officials say the first strike hit a shopping complex in Kryvyi Rih at about 16:30 local time on August 21. A large fire broke out inside the building, where shops were operating and civilians were present.

Reuters reported that a second drone struck approximately 30 minutes later. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy described the sequence as a “double-tap” attack and said emergency responders were among those placed at risk by the second wave. Ukraine’s Office of the Prosecutor General also said two drone strikes hit the shopping centre during the afternoon.

The shopping complex has been identified in Ukrainian reporting as Sonyachna Halereia. Images and video released by Ukrainian authorities and news organizations showed heavy smoke, damaged commercial space, firefighters and rescue crews working through the wreckage.

By late evening, local authorities had reported 15 deaths and 130 injuries. Shortly after midnight, regional officials said a sixteenth body had been recovered.

Why the second strike matters

The phrase “double-tap strike” refers to a tactic in which an initial attack is followed by another strike after rescuers, medical teams, police, journalists or civilians have arrived. Ukrainian officials have repeatedly accused Russian forces of using this pattern during the war.

In this case, the claim is based on the timing described by Ukrainian authorities: one drone hit the shopping centre, emergency services began responding, and another drone arrived roughly half an hour later. The Institute for the Study of War said geolocated footage was consistent with a second strike at the site.

The intent behind a strike is harder to establish than its timing. Ukrainian officials, including Zelenskyy and Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha, said the second attack was deliberate and aimed at maximizing casualties among civilians and rescuers. That is an allegation of intent made by the Ukrainian government. The publicly available evidence confirms the sequence of two attacks but does not by itself independently establish the decision-making process behind them.

The human toll

The scale of the injuries makes this one of the most serious attacks on Kryvyi Rih in recent months. Officials said 23 children were among the wounded. Twenty-nine people were reported in serious condition, including five children, and dozens remained hospitalized overnight.

Emergency crews continued searching the site after the fire was brought under control. Local authorities said some people remained unaccounted for during the rescue phase, another reason the casualty count could still move.

Kryvyi Rih is a major industrial city in central Ukraine and the hometown of President Zelenskyy. It has been repeatedly targeted during the war. In April 2025, a Russian missile strike on the city killed 20 people, including nine children, according to Ukrainian authorities.

What happened elsewhere the same day

The Kryvyi Rih attack was part of a broader day of Russian strikes across Ukraine. Reuters reported a separate drone attack in the Mykolaiv region that killed four people, including three children. Other attacks were reported in the Kharkiv region.

Ukraine, meanwhile, has continued striking Russian military and energy infrastructure with long-range drones. The two sides are now operating in a war environment where deep strikes, energy disruption and drone attacks are routine, even as diplomatic efforts continue in parallel.

That wider context does not diminish the significance of the shopping-centre attack. The central fact remains that a civilian commercial site was hit during the day and a very large number of people were killed or wounded.

Political and legal implications

Zelenskyy condemned the attack and said Ukraine would respond. Sybiha called for stronger sanctions against Russia’s military-industrial sector and additional air-defense support for Ukraine.

Under international humanitarian law, parties to a conflict must distinguish between military targets and civilians, and they must take precautions to minimize civilian harm. Deliberately attacking civilians or rescue personnel can constitute a war crime. Determining criminal responsibility requires evidence about target selection, intent, command structures and the circumstances of each strike.

Ukraine’s prosecutor’s office has opened investigations into thousands of alleged war crimes since the full-scale invasion began. The Kryvyi Rih attack is likely to be documented within that broader legal effort, particularly because of the alleged second strike after emergency services arrived.

What remains uncertain

The final casualty count is not yet settled. Rescue operations were continuing when this report was prepared, and officials had warned that the number of victims could rise.

The exact drone models used in both strikes also require caution. Some Ukrainian officials described the second aircraft as a jet-powered Shahed-type drone, but technical identification from debris had not been independently published in full by early August 22.

There is also no publicly available independent evidence establishing who selected the shopping centre as a target or the precise objective of the second strike. Ukraine says the attacks were intentional strikes on civilians and rescuers; that claim should be distinguished from the independently verifiable facts about timing, location and casualties.

What happens next

The immediate priority is the rescue operation, treatment of the injured and identification of victims. Regional and city authorities are expected to keep updating casualty figures as hospitals and rescue teams complete their work.

Internationally, the attack will add pressure to Ukraine’s calls for more air-defense interceptors and tighter sanctions. European leaders are also preparing for another meeting of the “Coalition of the Willing” on August 24, where support for Ukraine is expected to be discussed.

For Kryvyi Rih, the event is another mass-casualty strike in a city that has lived under recurring missile and drone attacks for years. The second-wave allegation makes this episode particularly significant because it raises a separate question beyond the initial strike: whether the emergency response itself was deliberately placed in the target zone.
