Modern Warfare 4 beta: new movement, maps and open beta dates

Infinity Ward is testing a faster, more fluid version of grounded Call of Duty multiplayer, with Korea-inspired maps, new traversal mechanics and a public beta beginning August 28.

By VISIONE Wire · August 24, 2026 · 20:01 CEST

Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 is in its first beta window, giving preorder players an early look at Infinity Ward's rebuilt movement system, multiplayer maps and weapon handling before a wider public open beta begins on August 28. The current early-access period runs through August 25 on Xbox Series X|S, PlayStation 5 and PC. The second beta weekend, scheduled for August 28 through September 1, is open to all players on supported platforms and includes Nintendo Switch 2.

The timing gives Activision a concentrated test just as gamescom week begins in Cologne. More importantly, it gives players their first chance to judge whether Infinity Ward can pull off the central design promise behind Modern Warfare 4 multiplayer: speed up decision-making and traversal without turning the game into an exaggerated mobility shooter.

What is confirmed about the beta

Xbox Wire lists two beta windows. Weekend one began August 21 and ends August 25, with access tied to eligible preorders. Weekend two begins August 28 and ends September 1 and is described as open to all players across Xbox Series X|S, PlayStation 5, PC and Nintendo Switch 2.

Activision's own multiplayer briefing confirms that six Core 6v6 maps are planned for the beta, alongside a selection of modes and experimental systems. Search and Destroy is confirmed for beta play, while new and returning modes include Hijack, Team Deathmatch, Domination and other staples from the series.

The full game is scheduled for October 23, 2026. Infinity Ward leads development, with Beenox responsible for the optimized PC version and Digital Legends developing the Nintendo Switch 2 edition natively.

The movement system is the biggest mechanical change

Infinity Ward is framing Modern Warfare 4 as a boots-on-the-ground game, but the phrase understates how much traversal is changing. The studio says the system is designed around responsiveness, control and preserving momentum rather than simply increasing raw movement speed.

New mechanics include Mantle Slide, which lets players flow directly from climbing over an obstacle into a slide; Mantle Momentum, which preserves forward velocity across obstacles; Mantle Steering, which allows directional control during a mantle; and the Supine Slide, a longer low slide that ends with the operator on their back.

Vertical movement also expands through pipe climbing, fast descents on pipes, ledge shimmies and directional jumps from ladders and hanging positions. Infinity Ward says these mechanics are intended to work across Campaign, Multiplayer, DMZ and Warzone, making them a broader systems change rather than a multiplayer-only experiment.

The practical effect is that maps can contain more deliberate alternate routes. Xbox Wire's interview with the development team describes pipes, windows, vehicle gaps and low openings being placed or retrofitted to support flanking paths. On the Silkworm map, players can use a mantle slide across cars while keeping their weapon ready; on Lithium, a supine slide can pass beneath partially open garage doors.

Why Infinity Ward says it removed some cover

One of the more counterintuitive design choices is the decision to reduce cover in some spaces. Multiplayer Creative Director Geoff Smith told Xbox Wire that fewer cover points can make play feel faster without simply increasing character speed, because players have fewer angles and hiding places to check as they move through a lane.

That idea connects directly to the studio's emphasis on clearer sightlines. Modern Warfare games have historically balanced photoreal environments against competitive readability, and the new maps are being built around a rule that visual storytelling should not interfere with combat clarity.

Art Director Sergio Gill said the team uses photogrammetry and environmental scans to build believable spaces, but gameplay remains the final constraint. That matters especially in dense urban environments inspired by South Korea, where signs, storefronts, digital billboards and street furniture can easily create visual noise.

South Korea is central to the multiplayer identity

The Korean Peninsula is a major setting for Modern Warfare 4's story, and multiplayer draws heavily from that visual direction. Infinity Ward says teams researched contemporary South Korean culture, including K-pop, PC bangs, esports, food, fashion and coastal architecture.

Scan teams traveled to South Korea to capture reference material and help the art team build a library of surfaces, structures and environmental details. The result appears in maps such as Silkworm, a South Korean city-inspired location, and Lotus, a coastal Korean fishing village damaged by an amphibious assault.

Infinity Ward is not treating those maps as literal one-to-one recreations. Doors, windows, streets and other objects are adjusted to support Call of Duty's player scale and movement rules. The studio describes that as a necessary trade-off between authenticity and competitive map design.

Modern Warfare 4 launches with 12 Core maps

Activision says the finished multiplayer package will launch with 12 Core 6v6 maps, in addition to Gunfight spaces, large-scale modes and the modular Kill Block environment. Six of those Core maps are planned for the beta.

Kill Block is one of the more unusual ideas. Set inside the Westbridge Training Facility, it is assembled from modular sections that can reconfigure between rounds. Activision says more than 500 combinations are possible at launch, changing sightlines, cover and routes as matches progress.

The broader launch mode list includes Team Deathmatch, Domination, Hardpoint, Kill Confirmed, Free for All and Search and Destroy, plus new modes such as Counter Attack, Inflation and Hijack. Ground War returns with Combat Outpost and additional large-player-count experiences.

Gunplay is being rebuilt around consistency

Movement is the headline feature, but Infinity Ward is also changing how weapons are presented and handled. Activision says Modern Warfare 4 removes bloom from the core accuracy model, aiming to make shot placement more predictable and more directly tied to player input.

The studio has also unified the weapon and environmental field of view, rather than rendering the weapon at a substantially different FOV from the world. On PC, additional lens and field-of-view options are intended to improve awareness without shrinking targets excessively.

Weapon animation changes depending on movement, nearby geometry and operator stance. The design goal is to make guns feel physical without allowing visual effects to obscure the information players need during a firefight.

What the beta still cannot answer

The current test can show whether the movement feels intuitive and whether the maps remain readable under real player pressure, but it cannot settle every question about the final game. Balance will change, spawn logic can be adjusted and the final network environment will be much larger than a beta population.

There is also a difference between a mechanic feeling exciting during a short test and remaining healthy across months of competitive play. Mantle slides, pipe routes and modular spaces could create meaningful tactical depth, or they could produce dominant movement patterns once high-level players optimize every route.

Performance on Nintendo Switch 2 is another major question. The platform joins the public beta during the second weekend, providing the first large-scale opportunity to compare responsiveness and visual quality against the Xbox, PlayStation and PC versions.

Bottom line

Fact: Modern Warfare 4's early-access beta runs through August 25, while the public open beta is scheduled for August 28 through September 1. The full game launches October 23, 2026.

Fact: Infinity Ward has introduced new mantle, slide, climbing and ledge mechanics while emphasizing clearer sightlines, grounded movement and more predictable gunplay.

Analysis: The beta is testing a difficult compromise. Infinity Ward wants movement to feel freer and faster without abandoning the grounded identity associated with Modern Warfare. The most important question over the next week is not whether the new mechanics look spectacular in clips, but whether they create readable, learnable combat once millions of players begin exploiting them.
