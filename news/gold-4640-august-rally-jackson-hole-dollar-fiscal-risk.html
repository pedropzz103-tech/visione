Gold climbs above $4,640 as August rally nears 15%

The precious metal is extending one of its strongest monthly advances in years as a weaker dollar, US fiscal anxiety, geopolitical tension and expectations around Jackson Hole push investors toward defensive assets.

By VISIONE Wire · August 24, 2026 · 08:24 CEST

Gold rose above $4,640 an ounce on Monday, August 24, extending a powerful August rally as investors moved toward assets perceived as protection against currency weakness, fiscal uncertainty and geopolitical risk.

Spot gold was up about 0.8% near $4,641 an ounce in early trading, according to Reuters, while US gold futures were around $4,697. The move followed a sharp advance late last week and put the metal close to a 15% gain for August so far.

The scale of that rise is the important part of the story. Gold is no longer merely reacting to one inflation print, one central-bank meeting or one geopolitical headline. Several forces are now pulling in the same direction: a softer dollar, anxiety about US debt and Treasury-market intervention, renewed tension around Iran and the Strait of Hormuz, and uncertainty over how the Federal Reserve will frame policy at Jackson Hole.

Gold's August move has accelerated quickly

The latest leg of the rally began to gather momentum after gold reclaimed the $4,500 area last week. Reuters reported that spot gold jumped more than 2% on Friday and reached its highest level in more than three months. By Monday morning it had pushed further, above $4,640.

That rise has transformed August into an unusually strong month for the metal. Reuters calculated the month-to-date gain at roughly 15% on Monday, while separate market reporting over the weekend put the increase above 13% before the latest move.

Those figures can shift during the session, so they should not be read as a final monthly return. The confirmed point is that gold has staged a double-digit advance in August and is trading at levels not seen since May.

The broader precious-metals complex has also been firm. Reuters put silver near $69 an ounce on Monday, platinum around $1,879 and palladium close to $1,350. Gold, however, is carrying the clearest macro signal because of its sensitivity to currencies, real yields and systemic risk.

A weaker dollar is making gold more attractive

Gold is priced globally in US dollars. When the dollar weakens, the metal becomes cheaper in other currencies, which can support demand outside the United States. That relationship is not mechanical on every trading day, but it is a major part of the current move.

The dollar has been under pressure as investors reassess US fiscal conditions and the Treasury's approach to long-term debt markets. A softer dollar has therefore added a currency tailwind at the same time that investors are looking for alternatives to government bonds and cash.

This is where the rally becomes more than a traditional safe-haven response. Gold is increasingly being discussed as part of a broader trade against currency debasement and fiscal deterioration: investors buy assets with limited supply when they become less confident about the long-run purchasing power of fiat currencies or the stability of sovereign debt markets.

That interpretation is still an investor thesis, not a settled fact. But it helps explain why gold has remained strong even when individual risk headlines fade.

US fiscal concerns are now part of the gold story

The Treasury market has become a central source of volatility in 2026. Long-dated US yields have remained elevated, and the Treasury has expanded efforts to buy back longer-term government debt in an attempt to improve market functioning and reduce pressure in parts of the curve.

Those actions can support bond-market liquidity, but they have also intensified debate about the sustainability of US borrowing and the credibility of fiscal policy. When investors perceive that governments may rely more heavily on financial intervention to stabilize debt markets, gold can benefit from the search for assets outside that system.

That does not mean Treasury buybacks automatically cause gold to rise. The relationship runs through expectations for yields, inflation, currency values and confidence. The current market simply happens to be combining all four.

Higher bond yields would normally be a headwind for gold because the metal does not pay interest. Yet the latest rally shows that investors are also pricing the quality and durability of those yields. A high nominal return is less attractive if investors simultaneously fear inflation, currency weakness or fiscal stress.

Geopolitical risk is adding another layer

Gold's move is also unfolding against renewed US-Iran tension. Washington is preparing additional sanctions on Iran, while markets continue to monitor the Strait of Hormuz because of its importance to global energy flows.

Oil prices eased on Monday, but the geopolitical risk has not disappeared. Brent crude remained above $93 a barrel in early trading, and investors are watching whether sanctions or retaliation could again restrict energy supply.

For gold, that matters in two ways. First, geopolitical escalation can create direct demand for safe-haven assets. Second, higher energy prices can feed inflation, complicating the Federal Reserve's policy path and potentially reducing confidence in fixed-income returns.

The Iran story is therefore not the sole cause of the rally, but it is reinforcing an already supportive environment.

Jackson Hole is the next major test

The next scheduled macro catalyst is the Federal Reserve's Jackson Hole Economic Policy Symposium. The Federal Reserve's official calendar lists Chair Kevin Warsh for keynote remarks on Friday, August 28.

Markets will listen for clues about how the Fed views inflation, real interest rates, financial conditions and the balance sheet. Investors should be careful not to assume that Warsh will provide explicit guidance on the September policy decision. Reuters noted that his preference for limited forward guidance could leave traders with fewer direct signals than they want.

The timing still matters. Gold is highly sensitive to expectations for real rates. A more hawkish message could lift yields and the dollar, challenging the rally. A cautious or dovish tone could do the opposite.

Before then, investors are also waiting for US inflation data, including the July personal consumption expenditures report. That release could reshape the rate outlook before Warsh speaks.

What is confirmed and what remains uncertain

The confirmed picture on Monday morning is straightforward: gold is trading above $4,640 an ounce, up sharply in August, while the dollar remains weak and investors are focused on fiscal risk, geopolitics and the Fed.

What is not confirmed is whether this rally can hold through the end of the month. Gold has moved quickly, which raises the possibility of profit-taking. A stronger dollar, higher real yields or a reduction in geopolitical tension could reverse part of the advance.

There is also a technical question. Rapid moves can attract momentum traders and options activity, which can amplify both gains and reversals. That means a fundamentally supportive environment can still produce violent short-term pullbacks.

Why this move matters beyond precious metals

Gold's August surge is a signal about investor confidence across several markets at once. It sits at the intersection of currencies, government debt, inflation expectations and geopolitical risk.

If gold continues higher alongside rising bond yields, it would suggest investors are becoming more concerned about fiscal credibility and inflation than about the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset. If gold weakens while yields rise and the dollar strengthens, the rally may prove to have been more tactical.

Either outcome will matter well beyond commodity desks. The same forces influencing gold are also moving technology shares, cryptocurrencies, sovereign bonds and global currencies.

For now, the metal has become one of the clearest expressions of the market's defensive mood. Above $4,640 and up roughly 15% in August, gold is telling investors that the debate over debt, dollars, inflation and geopolitical risk is no longer happening in separate rooms. It has merged into one trade.
