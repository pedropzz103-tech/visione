Samsung Odyssey G6 hits 1,100Hz as 2027 gaming monitor lineup debuts

Samsung is pushing gaming displays into four-digit refresh rates while expanding OLED into larger and more flexible formats, with its new Odyssey range making its public debut at Gamescom 2026.

By VISIONE Wire · Published August 27, 2026 · 12:08 CEST

Samsung Electronics has unveiled its 2027 Odyssey gaming monitor lineup at Gamescom 2026, led by a 27-inch Odyssey G6 that can reach up to 1,100Hz in HD mode. The company is also introducing a 49-inch OLED G9, a 32-inch OLED G8 and what it describes as the world's first 42-inch 16:9 curved OLED gaming monitor, the Odyssey OLED G7.

The announcement matters because refresh-rate competition has moved rapidly beyond the 240Hz and 360Hz displays that defined high-end esports monitors only a few product cycles ago. Samsung's G6 pushes that race into four digits, but the specification comes with an important trade-off: the maximum 1,100Hz mode runs at HD resolution. In QHD, the same monitor tops out at 600Hz.

Samsung announced the lineup on August 27 during Gamescom in Cologne, Germany. Pricing has not yet been disclosed for the new OLED models, and availability will vary by market. The company says the Odyssey G6 will launch globally in the second half of 2026.

The 1,100Hz Odyssey G6 is built around a resolution trade-off

The Odyssey G6, model G60H, uses a 27-inch Fast IPS panel with a 1ms response time. Its Dual Mode lets players choose between up to 600Hz at QHD resolution and up to 1,100Hz at HD resolution. Samsung is explicitly positioning that second mode for competitive first-person shooters, where motion clarity and input response can matter more than pixel density.

The figure is striking, but a higher refresh rate does not automatically translate into an equally large improvement for every player. To benefit from a 1,100Hz panel, a game and PC still need to produce extremely high frame rates, and diminishing perceptual returns become part of the equation as refresh rates rise. The headline number therefore makes the most sense for elite competitive scenarios rather than as a universal target for mainstream gaming.

What is confirmed is the final product specification. Samsung says the G6 had previously been shown at CES 2026, but the launch version raises its maximum refresh rate to 1,100Hz. The company also says the monitor will be available globally during the second half of this year.

OLED G9 combines 49 inches, 5120 x 1440 and 360Hz

The Odyssey OLED G9, model G95SJ, is a 49-inch Dual QHD display with a 5120 x 1440 resolution, 360Hz refresh rate and 0.03ms gray-to-gray response time. Samsung calls it the world's first 49-inch DQHD gaming monitor to reach 360Hz.

The panel uses Samsung's fifth-generation QD-OLED technology with what the company calls Penta Tandem construction. Samsung claims the five-layer panel is up to 1.3 times more efficient and offers up to twice the lifetime of the previous generation. Those durability and efficiency figures are manufacturer claims rather than independent test results, so real-world validation will have to wait for production hardware.

Dual Mode allows the G9 to switch from DQHD at 360Hz to 2560 x 720 Dual HD at up to 720Hz. That gives the ultrawide display a similar philosophy to the G6: preserve resolution for cinematic or broad-field gameplay, then sacrifice pixels when speed becomes the priority.

The OLED G8 offers three speed tiers

Samsung's 32-inch Odyssey OLED G8, model G80SJ, uses a 4K QD-OLED panel and supports three resolution-refresh combinations: UHD at 360Hz, QHD at 520Hz and Full HD at 680Hz. It also carries a 0.03ms GtG response time.

Connectivity includes DisplayPort 2.1 UHBR20, two HDMI ports, USB-C with up to 98W Power Delivery, a two-port downstream USB hub and KVM support. Samsung says AMD FreeSync Premium Pro is supported, while Nvidia G-SYNC Compatible certification is planned for completion by launch.

The G8 also uses Samsung's Glare Free treatment. The company says the film is 54% less glossy than conventional anti-reflection film. Again, that percentage comes from Samsung's own comparison methodology, so buyers should treat it as a manufacturer specification rather than an independent measurement.

A 42-inch curved OLED G7 targets cinematic PC gaming

The Odyssey OLED G7, model G75SJ, takes a different route. Instead of chasing the highest possible refresh rate, it pairs a 42-inch 4K OLED panel with a 1000R curve, 165Hz refresh rate and 0.03ms GtG response time.

Samsung calls the product the world's first 42-inch 16:9 curved OLED gaming monitor. The company says its screen area is roughly 72% larger than the previous 32-inch model. The size and curvature position it closer to a desk-scale cinematic display for RPGs, racing games and open-world titles than to a conventional esports screen.

That division within the lineup is deliberate. The G6 is the speed specialist, the G9 combines ultrawide immersion with unusually high refresh, the G8 attempts to cover several performance modes in one 32-inch format, and the G7 stretches OLED gaming toward a much larger curved 16:9 canvas.

Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 is part of Samsung's HDR10+ push

Samsung is also expanding its HDR10+ GAMING program. At Gamescom, the company highlighted a partnership with Activision Blizzard around Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4, saying HDR10+ GAMING is being integrated into the development process for compatible displays.

Samsung plans to expand the HDR10+ GAMING ecosystem to more than 20 AAA titles by the first half of 2027. It is also growing support for Odyssey 3D through direct collaboration with game studios, with Cyberpunk 2077 and Cronos: The New Dawn among the titles being demonstrated at the event.

Those partnerships matter because premium monitor features increasingly depend on software support. A panel can advertise higher brightness, variable refresh rates, 3D effects or dynamic HDR, but the quality of the experience often depends on how well games, drivers and display firmware communicate.

What is confirmed and what is still unknown

Confirmed by Samsung: the G6 reaches up to 1,100Hz at HD and 600Hz at QHD; the OLED G9 is a 49-inch DQHD 360Hz display; the OLED G8 offers UHD 360Hz, QHD 520Hz and FHD 680Hz modes; and the OLED G7 is a 42-inch 4K curved OLED display running at 165Hz.

Also confirmed: the G6 is planned for global availability in the second half of 2026, while timing for the wider 2027 Odyssey lineup will vary by market. Samsung has not announced pricing for the new lineup.

Still uncertain: regional prices, exact launch dates for each OLED model, independent measurements of brightness and panel longevity, and how much practical benefit the four-digit refresh mode will provide outside highly optimized competitive gaming systems.

Manufacturer claims awaiting independent testing: Samsung's efficiency and lifetime improvements for the fifth-generation QD-OLED panel, the glare-reduction comparison and the broader performance advantages implied by its new display modes.

Why four-digit refresh rates matter even if most players never use them

The move to 1,100Hz is partly a showcase of panel engineering. Most current games, GPUs and players will not operate at four-digit frame rates, especially at visually demanding settings. But extreme specifications often migrate downward over time, influencing panel response, controller electronics and lower-cost products even when the flagship mode remains niche.

The more interesting trend across Samsung's announcement may be flexibility. Rather than forcing buyers to choose one fixed balance between resolution and speed, several of the new Odyssey models can change modes. That reflects a PC market where the same display may be used for a competitive shooter one hour, a 4K single-player game the next and productivity work afterward.

It also shows how gaming-monitor differentiation is shifting. Resolution alone is no longer enough at the premium end. Manufacturers are competing on refresh rate, OLED structure, subpixel layout, anti-glare coatings, HDR behavior, USB-C power, KVM functions and software partnerships.

Bottom line

Samsung's Gamescom 2026 Odyssey announcement is a speed race wrapped inside a broader display strategy. The 1,100Hz G6 supplies the headline, but the 49-inch 360Hz OLED G9, triple-mode OLED G8 and 42-inch curved OLED G7 show a lineup built around very different kinds of high-end play.

The decisive missing number is price. Once Samsung publishes regional pricing and firm launch dates, buyers will be able to judge whether the 2027 Odyssey range is merely a technology showcase or a realistic upgrade path. Until then, the specifications make one thing clear: the gaming-monitor refresh-rate ceiling has moved again.
